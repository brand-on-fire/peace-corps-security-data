# Public Peace Corps country hazard observations

This repository maintains public-source observations for reviewed active Peace Corps countries. It is an independent policy research dataset, not an official Peace Corps service or a determination that a place is safe.

`docs/data/snapshot.json` contains the current 30-day view. `docs/data/history.json` is a selected-field index; complete reports and revisions are in the country files under `docs/data/history/`. Country manifests may list multiple complete-record parts. No Volunteer names, private locations or postings are collected.

Multipart countries also publish a bounded `history/CC-index.json` routing index. It binds the complete ordered incident list and exact part sizes/hashes to the snapshot publication revision, so the browser can validate and fetch only the requested complete part. Full source text, polygons and evidence revisions remain in the original records. The routing index is capped at 2 MiB and 999 parts; existing file, browser and storage limits are unchanged. Older publications without this index retain the bounded legacy reader behavior.

The `security-weather-v2` public scope excludes routine CAP weather, reviewed contradictory CAP severity narratives, and low-impact GDACS fire/cyclone/drought notices without verified relevant human impact. Exclusions are recorded in the publication's `collector.weatherExclusions` ledger with source identities and retained evidence hashes. Complete excluded observations remain in `retained-weather` for correction and lifecycle reconciliation; they are not silently deleted. This is a reviewed inclusion policy, not proof of universal hazard coverage.

`docs/data/discovery.json` separates held headlines from accepted source records. Five reviewed English publisher profiles can admit complete-source security reports when event, place and date checks pass. Negated events, simulations, planned maintenance, ambiguous locations and unsupported accounts stay in review. Explicit prior reviews take precedence. A publisher's country or publication time alone does not establish the event location or date. Full news bodies are not republished.

Caribe Actual's reviewed Spanish crime collector requests checks every 30 minutes. It examines the complete latest 100 posts and rechecks up to three older accepted post IDs per cycle, with a 90-second source deadline. Supported homicide, injurious shooting and dated robbery accounts retain Spanish headlines, original evidence, and short attributed English factual summaries. Evidence remains reported and location country-only. Unsupported updates are held; verified inaccessible articles retain their earlier evidence. Failed or pending correction checks preserve the last complete-source success time while permitting validated new reports to publish. This is regional publisher coverage, not complete Costa Rican crime reporting or a one-year archive.

The workflow runs only in the public `brand-on-fire/peace-corps-security-data` repository on its default branch. It uses standard Ubuntu runners, the built-in ephemeral repository token, public HTTP feeds and no Actions caches or artifacts. Official hazards and five automatic news profiles request 15-minute checks; other sources use their reviewed 30-minute, one-hour or six-hour policies. GitHub schedules can be delayed or dropped. Source health and dates are part of the data.

Two additional reviewed official adapters have narrower schedules and coverage:

- **Nepal BIPAD:** hourly public API checks for verified, approved disaster records with positive actual impact under reviewed hazard profiles. Reported-date and validated newest-modification ordering provide overlapping catch-up windows; the provider ignores modified-date filters, so the adapter does not use them. Rain, storms, lightning and animal incidents remain outside the reviewed profiles. Separate loss-only edits may not change the parent incident's modification time; changes outside the overlap still require periodic retained-ID reconciliation. This correction gap remains open.
- **CDC travel notices:** six-hour checks limited to five reviewed factual-statement profiles: Zika in Indonesia, hepatitis A in Costa Rica, yellow fever in Colombia, chikungunya in Costa Rica, and Rocky Mountain spotted fever in Mexico. Notice-detail requests are serialized at least 20 seconds apart. New profiles, global/multi-country notices and unsupported changed statements require review. Original publication dates are preserved rather than treated as outbreak onset; older active notices remain in history and may be absent from the 30-day overview pending a current-advisory view. CDC levels are not incident severity, and expiration does not mean resolution. Source: CDC; original material is free and use does not imply CDC, HHS or U.S. government endorsement.

Neither adapter establishes complete country, province, district or category coverage. Their reviewed access, activation requirements and limitations are recorded in `config/source-reviews/2026-10-15-mvp-nepal-cdc.json`.

Country admission expires at the documented review date. A country needs a renewed, evidence-backed roster review to resume new observations. Current coverage excludes offshore hazard effects, does not establish every local-news source, and is not an emergency alert channel. Read each observation's source, precision, evidence status and dates.

The collector stops at explicit data-tree and repository-history growth limits rather than deleting the archive or using paid storage. Publication uses ordinary public Git files, readable at raw.githubusercontent.com. GitHub Pages is not enabled by this workflow.

## Collection health and scheduling

Collection runs in an isolated staging directory with an eight-minute deadline. Only a complete validated archive and snapshot replaces the last good publication; validation checks archived record identities, retained evidence, active-country scope, archive totals, and the browser content revision. A fatal failure or failure of every due source preserves the prior publication and writes only `docs/data/refresh-status.json`. Partial source outages retain prior records and are reported as degraded. A source producing a complete incident above the 12 MB record bound is deferred atomically before admission, without truncating it or blocking other valid sources.

The status document records the GitHub run, trigger, attempt, collection cycle, actual collector commit, source outcomes, last successful checks and a seven-day timing ledger. It evaluates all configured sources, including stale sources not due in that cycle. Each degraded cycle emits a runner warning; the run ends non-green if a cycle was degraded. Preflight, test and publication failures remain visible in Actions. No paid model, Cloudflare compute, cache or artifact service is used.

Natural schedules and reviewed code/configuration pushes use one bounded 285-minute collection window, attempting a cycle every 15 minutes. Manual runs perform one cycle. The next runner still depends on GitHub; neither a push nor a configured cron expression proves uninterrupted natural scheduling. Upstream changes, storage limits, validation errors, deadlines and failed publication stop the window without overwriting newer work. The requested handoff schedule remains minutes 11, 26, 41 and 56 each UTC hour.

`docs/data/publication.json` is a small revision hint. Readers must still validate the complete snapshot before accepting changed data. Source timestamps measure collection, not the time an event first became knowable. The WordPress profiles inspect a bounded latest-100-post window and retain an explicit coverage gap if it does not reach the previous successful check; this is not a one-year backfill or multilingual translation system.

Retained weather evidence uses lossless gzip storage under the explicit `retainedWeatherStorage: gzip-v1` policy. Complete source records and every original decoded byte are preserved, including older unreferenced parts. Collection, batch imports and public-content auditing validate the complete decoded representation; malformed, ambiguous and oversized files fail closed. Public incident files and the browser contract remain unchanged. The current-tree saving does not erase earlier Git objects.

Collector boundary files use lossless gzip storage; coordinates and source metadata are unchanged. The audit scans both compressed and decoded bytes. Original incident evidence and revisions remain in the complete archive. Storage ceilings stay at 500 MB for the public tree and 750 MB for repository history.

Current gaps include permitted current news discovery for Kyrgyz Republic, complete provincial/district reporting remits, broader non-English automatic extraction, specialized local health/transport/utility feeds, and verified offshore/cross-border hazard impacts. Ordinary CAP weather is excluded; a working national forecast feed is not treated as a replacement for a failed severe-warning feed. Source outages and held articles remain visible in the data.
